# Feedback: "Does learning to read your activations teach you to write them?"

Reviewer verdict: **keep with tweaks.** Cheap, natural, and feasible, but the modal outcome is a null on narrow LoRA detectors that readers will discount, the authors of both prior papers can scoop it in an afternoon, and two factual claims in the proposal are wrong or unverified (the ACB recordings are gated; the ACB code repo could not be inspected). The secondary analyses (layer-specific gain, reverse test) are weaker than the proposal presents.

What I checked (2026-11-14): arXiv 2608.21664, 2511.21399 and 2609.36490 (abstracts and full HTML of the first two), the LW post and all 8 comments, the Hugging Face API for the dataset, adapters and base models, OpenRouter prices. WebSearch was unavailable (session search budget exhausted), so the "has this been done" check rests on arXiv API queries, LW comments and HF listings only. github.com is blocked from this sandbox.

## 1. Would it get upvoted?

Probably not above 50 on the most likely outcome; plausibly yes on the less likely one.

- Reception of the direct ancestors is weak. The ACB post has exactly 50 karma and 8 comments. The Steering Awareness post (D7zQkrDjAKaa293EA) has 22 karma and 0 comments. The monitor-evasion post (adZq8a5s5K3FomyHC) has 14 karma and 0 comments. The high-karma posts in this cluster (130, 139, 219) are about whether introspection is real, not about activation control.
- A positive result ("introspection training measurably raises activation control; here is the size") would be cited in the "should we train models to introspect" argument that agastyasridharan and David Africa are having in the ACB comments. I would guess 50-90 karma.
- A null ("rank-32 LoRA detectors do not score higher on ACB") gets the reply "those adapters are geometric detectors of CAA vectors at one layer, not introspection; of course nothing transferred". The Steering Awareness paper itself says the mechanism is "a geometric detector rather than a generic anomaly detector" and that "detection and resistance are dissociable". I would guess 20-40 karma for the null alone.
- Who cares: the four ACB authors, people working on probes/latent monitors, and the introspection-is-dual-use debaters. That is a small audience.

## 2. Naturalness

Mainline. The ACB paper's discussion says a model "trained to modulate its activations the way models have been trained to detect steering ... could easily do much better" (verified in the arXiv HTML), and David Africa's comment of 2026-08-25 says "it'd be reasonable to do this + test other things like steering awareness". The quotes in section 1 of the proposal are accurate; the "worried about deliberately training models to introspect better" comment is also real (2026-08-27).

Two caveats:

- **Scoop risk is high and understated.** Joshua Fonseca Rivera and David Africa are authors of both the ACB and the Steering Awareness paper. They hold the adapters, the benchmark code and the raw recordings, and one of them said in public that this test is reasonable to do. `joshycodes` uploaded about twenty `*-control-*-sdf` models on Qwen3-32B and Gemma-4-31B in September, which looks like the SDF follow-up from the same comment thread being run. I found no published result, but the team should assume the authors may post one at any time and should move fast on a version the authors would not trivially produce (see section 5, dose-response).
- **Not already done, as far as I could check.** arXiv queries for "steering awareness", "activation control" + introspection, and "activation controllability" return only the three known papers plus one the proposal misses: arXiv 2610.20179, "Cross-Model In-Context Neurofeedback Reveals (Limited) Activation Control in LLMs" (2026-10-14). It tests ten off-the-shelf models and finds control no better than a second "writer" model achieves, i.e. little evidence of privileged access. It does not fine-tune for introspection, so it is not a duplicate, but it must be cited and it strengthens the validity objection to the ACB (section 3). Ji-An et al. 2505.13763 (metacognitive monitoring and control of activations) should also be cited.

## 3. Pre-mortem

How it plays out: day 1 the team finds the dataset is gated and the repo is either usable or not. Adapters load (they are ordinary PEFT repos with `adapter_config.json`, r=32, alpha=64, all attention and MLP projections, matching the paper's stated recipe, which raises my confidence that they are the paper's adapters). Detection verification passes for five or six pairs after some prompt-format debugging. ACB runs are quick. By day 2 they have paired S values with differences of mixed sign, about the size of the control-adapter differences, and a write-up that says "no evidence reading teaches writing, with caveats".

Rough probabilities:

| Outcome | Probability |
|---|---|
| Clear positive (adapter minus control positive in most pairs, scaling with detection) | 12% |
| Clear negative that is still interesting (tight nulls with verified detection and a working control) | 33% |
| Ambiguous mush (mixed signs, control adapters move S as much, direction drift) | 40% |
| Fails to run (no ACB code, cannot match published S, adapters fail verification) | 15% |

Specific failure modes, most likely first:

1. **Weak prior for transfer.** The adapters were trained on five fixed detection questions with a CAA vector added at the last prompt token at two-thirds depth, plus 50% Alpaca replay. The ACB prompt is "Write '{sentence}' exactly. Think about X." Nothing in the detection objective rewards producing a concept direction on instruction. The base rate for a narrow LoRA changing an unrelated instruction-following measure in a consistent direction is low.
2. **S is a poor primary outcome for a paired design.** S is a geometric mean of six link-transformed measures, two of which (Suppress, Layer Targeting) sit at p of about 0.5 for every model and contribute only noise. Gemma-2-9B's published S is 0.389 with a 95% CI of [0.350, 0.453], a width of 0.10. The proposal's dissociation criterion is "within 0.03 of zero"; a single pair will not resolve that unless the paired bootstrap is much tighter than the marginal one, which nobody has shown. Engage, Coverage and Temporal Control use D_ref set to 1.02 times the panel maximum (16.4, 6.90, 4.11), so the link saturates if an adapter exceeds the panel.
3. **d' normalisation.** Every measure divides by the no-instruction SD of the projection. Steering-aware adapters stay fluent under perturbation and are "more susceptible to steering"; that plausibly changes baseline variance. A change in the denominator would masquerade as changed control. The proposal lists raw effect sizes as a mitigation; they need to be the headline, not a footnote.
4. **Differential trial retention.** ACB keeps only trials with transcription similarity of at least 0.85. Gemma-2-9B retains 86.5% and Llama 3.1 8B 88.2% in the main battery. Adapters may retain a different subset (some may emit detection-style responses), so base and adapter are compared on different trials unless the analysis restricts to trials both retain.
5. **Gated data.** `joshycodes/activation-control-battery` is `gated: manual` ("Access is gated while the paper is unpublished; request access and the maintainers will approve"); file requests return 401. The proposal says "Public and verified". Agents cannot rely on a manual approval. Step 2 as written (compare with the released recording) is blocked; the fallback is the per-model numbers in the paper's Table 1 (Gemma 2 9B: Engage 5.55, Suppress 0.54, Dial 1.00, Temporal 5.02, Coverage 1.31, Layer Targeting 0.017, S 0.389).
6. **Code not verified.** The repo URL is in the arXiv header but nobody in this pipeline has seen its contents. If it is missing or incomplete, the ACB must be rebuilt from Appendix A and C (sentences, baseline words, D_ref values and formulas are all in the paper, so about half a day), and "matches published S" becomes the gate.
7. **Only one adapter base overlaps the ACB panel.** Gemma-2-9B is in the panel. Llama 3.1 8B is in the panel but the adapter is on Llama 3 8B; Qwen2.5-7B, DeepSeek-7B, Qwen2.5-32B, QwQ-32B and Llama-3-70B have no published ACB numbers. Six of seven pairs have no external check on the base run.
8. **The layer-specific analysis is close to dead on arrival.** The ACB layer-targeting sweep covers only the second half of the network. Gemma L10 (24% depth) and Llama L8 (25%) are outside it and L16 (50%) is at the edge. The Steering Awareness paper's layer ablation marks an early-layer condition as degenerate (100% false positives: the model always claims detection). More basically, being trained to detect an injection at layer n gives the model no mapping from the token "layer 21" to a depth. I put under 3% on a diagonal appearing. It is cheap, but it should not be advertised as "the most interesting single result".
9. **The reverse test is confounded.** The concept word is in the conversation, so naming it shows nothing; and the adapters were trained with "mismatch" and "text priming" negatives, i.e. explicitly trained to say no when a concept is merely mentioned in text. The expected result is "no", and a "yes" would be uninterpretable without a matched control in which the concept is mentioned but not to be thought about.
10. **Base-model gating.** `google/gemma-2-9b-it`, `meta-llama/Meta-Llama-3-8B-Instruct` and `-70B-Instruct` are manually gated. Ungated mirrors exist (`unsloth/gemma-2-9b-it`, `unsloth/llama-3-8b-Instruct`, `NousResearch/Meta-Llama-3-8B-Instruct`, `NousResearch/Meta-Llama-3-70B-Instruct`); the plan should name them.
11. **Unsurprising either way.** A null is what the Steering Awareness paper's own "geometric detector" and "detection does not confer resistance" findings predict. A small positive shared with control adapters is a statement about fine-tuning.

## 4. Cost check

The estimate is realistic and probably generous. The 0.7 GPU-h per run average is dominated by 70B-744B models; 7-9B runs will be faster. Checked: claude-haiku-5.5 is $0.10/$0.50 per M on OpenRouter. I did not re-check the RunPod prices (NOTES gives H100 PCIe $2.89/h, H200 $5.29/h). Items the estimate omits: storage for activations (the released Gemma-size recordings are tens of GB per run; with about 40 runs, budget a network volume or record only projections), and adapter retraining for the dose-response control I recommend below (the paper reports about 4 h on an A100 per model, so about $10-20 for one 9B model). My estimate: $180-300. The $260 figure stands. Dropping the Llama-3-70B pair saves about $40 and one infra risk for little loss.

## 5. Recommended changes (most important first)

1. **Add a within-model dose-response, and make it the centrepiece.** Retrain steering awareness on Gemma-2-9B (and Qwen2.5-7B, which is ungated) with the paper's recipe, saving checkpoints through training (the paper reports validation detection going 0% to 70% in epoch 1 and 80-90% by epoch 3), plus an Alpaca-only run with identical steps as the control. Run the ACB at each checkpoint. S or Engage tracking detection within one model, and not tracking steps in the control, is far more convincing than seven cross-model pairs with one point each, and it is the version the original authors cannot produce by loading two existing adapters. No training code URL appears in the paper, so budget half a day to reimplement from Appendix B and E (prompts, templates, hyperparameters and dataset sizes are all given).
2. **Fix the asset claims.** State that the ACB recordings are gated and unavailable; reproduce Gemma-2-9B against the paper's Table 1 values. Add a day-1 gate: if the repo is unusable and a reimplementation cannot match Table 1 for Gemma-2-9B within the published CI, stop. Name the ungated base-model mirrors.
3. **Change the primary outcome.** Pre-specify raw (un-normalised) Engage and Dial Rank differences, on trials retained by both models, with projections onto the base model's concept directions, as primary. Report S as secondary. Report baseline SD and retention rate per model beside every d'. Replace the "within 0.03" criterion with an equivalence bound derived from the base model's run-to-run variability (run the Gemma-2-9B base twice with different seeds to measure it).
4. **Add the direct positive control the paper's sentence actually calls for.** Train one small LoRA on Gemma-2-9B to write (maximise the concept projection under "think about X" on training concepts, test on the ten ACB concepts). This shows what a real gain on the ACB looks like, calibrates the size of any reading-to-writing transfer, and gives the reverse direction (does write-training improve detection?) that the proposal defers to stage 2. It also guarantees the post has one positive result. About $15.
5. **Demote the layer-targeting and reverse tests** to appendix checks, state the expected nulls and why, and for the reverse test add the matched control (concept mentioned in the copied instruction but with no "think about" request) and score detection only, not naming.
6. **Shrink the model list.** Four pairs with the best detection and no gating trouble (Gemma-2-9B 90.8%, Qwen2.5-7B 85.5%, Qwen2.5-32B 95.5%, QwQ-32B 94.8%) are enough. Llama-3-8B (43.3% detection, 8.1% base rate) and DeepSeek-7B (50.0%) dilute the comparison; Llama-3-70B adds cost and infra risk. Spend the saved time on items 1 and 4.
7. **Cite and position against arXiv 2610.20179 and 2505.13763**, and say plainly in the write-up that a null speaks to LoRA-trained CAA detectors, not to introspection in general.
8. **Check HF and LW for an author result before starting and again before posting.** If the authors publish base-versus-adapter ACB numbers first, items 1 and 4 are what remains novel.

If the funder has to choose inside this cluster, this proposal is the cheapest and the lowest in execution risk, but it has the weakest expected reception unless changes 1 and 4 are made. With them, it is worth funding at about $260.
