# Feedback: "Do independently trained NLAs agree?" (nla/02)

Verdict: **major overhaul**. The question is reasonable and I found no prior measurement of it, but the "relatedness ladder" that the whole design rests on does not exist in the public checkpoints as described, and the main ground truth (truth about the source text) cannot distinguish "the activation is speaking" from "easy facts recur".

## 1. Would it get upvoted?

Unlikely to clear 50. The cluster's four posts scored 209, 125, 82, 56, but they are the original paper, a Greenblatt post, a TurnTrout post and the first cheap follow-up. Since mid-July, every NLA follow-up I could find on LessWrong scored between 3 and 31 (Matryoshka 31, J-space 29, monitors 28, internalized behaviours 25, reconstructor-not-semantic 20, deletion gap 3, reward fix 7). The closest analogue in kind, a claim-level truth filter study, got 3. The topic is saturated and the audience has already absorbed "NLAs confabulate; reconstruction does not validate claims".

The version that could do well is a crisp positive with a practical recipe: "run two public NLAs, keep the intersection, precision goes from ~10% to >60%". Readers who use NLAs for auditing would care. But that headline is only interesting if the filtered claims are ones you could not have checked against the text anyway, which the plan does not establish (see section 3). The negative ("unrelated NLAs share false claims") would be a good complement to the init post, but cannot be supported with the available checkpoints. My estimate: about 10-15% chance of >50 karma if run as written, 25% if a clear positive lands on a state-level ground truth.

## 2. Naturalness

A moderately natural follow-up to the init post: "does the dependence on warm start show up as disagreement between NLAs" is a question its readers would ask. It is not what the authors named as next (they asked for larger models and data). The filter half is a port of the activation-oracle self-consistency trick; natural, and nobody seems to have done it for NLAs. It is opportunistic in a specific way: it is built around whichever checkpoints happen to be on Hugging Face, and those were not trained to vary one factor at a time. I found no evidence it has been done.

## 3. Pre-mortem

How it plays out: the agents spend most of day one getting five injection conventions to load (official SGLang path, nanoNLA additive LoRA, embedding replacement, norm-to-150, subfolder AR with a separate value head). Two of the eight variants have no published FVE and one has no AR, so "gate on reproducing FVE" silently becomes "the output looks like English". Generation is quick. The judge pipeline runs. The result table shows: last-token and theme claims recur everywhere; specific false details recur almost nowhere, including between two samples of the same AV. Cross-NLA and same-NLA filters both raise precision a lot and are within noise of each other.

Probabilities: clear positive 15%; clear negative that is still interesting 15%; ambiguous mush 55%; fails to run or is invalidated by a loading/matching bug 15%.

Specific failure reasons, most important first:

1. **The relatedness ladder is not there.** On Qwen2.5-7B, the "different warm start" NLA is a fine-tune of the official AV and is pre-RL, so the pair is parent/child. The other candidate has no AR and unknown provenance. On Qwen3-8B there is exactly one plausibly unrelated pair (nanoNLA vs marco-bazzani), which differs in a dozen ways at once (LoRA vs full fine-tune, injection method, 250 vs 400 RL steps, different FVE, AR frozen or not) and may share the same Sonnet prompt for the warm start. Any statement about "unrelated NLAs" is n=1 pair with no attribution to seed, warm start or recipe. The "sibling" level is confounded with explanation length: a 74-token explanation has fewer claims to recur.
2. **The outcome on analysis A is predictable and uninformative.** At temperature 1, confabulated specifics ("requiring a lengthy exer", invented quotes) are mostly sampling noise. They will be near the shuffled baseline already at level (i), same AV resampled. Then there is no room for a gradient across levels, and "false claims do not recur across NLAs" says nothing about warm-start artifacts. The init post's actual finding is about systematic style and content inherited from the warm start, which per-activation claim recurrence does not measure.
3. **Text-truth is the wrong label for the headline.** Claims are labelled against the source text. Recurrence correlates with truth through claim difficulty alone: generic and last-token claims are both easier to get right and easier to repeat. So "true claims recur several times more often" is close to guaranteed, with or without any faithful reading of the activation. Category stratification helps but does not fix it within a category. And a filter that selects text-true claims has little use: in deployment you have the text. The claims an auditor needs filtered are about internal state not visible in the text, which this design never labels.
4. **The next-token ground truth is thin.** "Expecting X" is the one thing the warm-start prompt explicitly asks for in the final paragraph, the AV can recompute it from recovered context, and a logit lens reads it for free. It guards against less than the proposal says.
5. **Claim matching is the fragile step and is under-specified.** The cost section labels one of four samples per variant but analysis A and B need truth labels for every sample that enters matching. One judge call is asked to cluster about 250 claims (8 variants x 4 samples x ~8 claims) with 1k output tokens, which is not enough to emit the clustering. Matching granularity (same theme vs same detail) is a free parameter that moves every number. If the matcher sees the source text it will merge true claims more readily than false ones.
6. **Power for the comparison that matters.** The interesting contrast is cross-NLA vs same-NLA resampling at equal budget and matched coverage. The expected difference is a few points of precision. With correlated claims inside activations and one pair of NLAs, that will not be resolved, and it does not generalise beyond that pair in any case.
7. **Base rates and judge.** Plausible-claim rates of 7-21% came from a lenient partial-credit rubric with Opus 4.8 on a 20k-document NLA. The official NLA and a strict three-way rubric will give different rates; quoting "7-21% to above 60%" as the target mixes rubrics.
8. **Analysis C is weak.** SFT-only `base` vs RL adapter moving toward or away from marco-bazzani's NLA is one model, one comparison, with length and FVE changing at the same time. It is not a replication of "plausibility falls during RL"; steps 3 already measures plausibility directly, which is the replication.

## 4. Cost check

The itemised arithmetic is right at the stated volumes, and prices are current. The volumes are understated: labelling all four samples rather than one is 48,000 calls, about $170 at Sonnet batch prices instead of $42; a matching protocol that actually works (pairwise entailment or chunked clustering) is roughly $100-150 instead of $60. Downloading about 150 GB of full fine-tunes and debugging five loaders will take more than the implied setup time, though 25 A100-hours is still about right. My estimate as written and done properly: **$330-400**. Using Haiku 5.5 for extraction and matching (validated against Sonnet on the 200-claim set) brings it back to about $150-200. It fits under $500 either way.

## 5. Recommended changes

1. **Fix the independent variable by training it.** Drop the claim that public checkpoints form a ladder. Move the stage-2 controlled runs into stage 1 on Qwen3-8B with EasyNLA and the public warm-start data: one seed-only twin and one NLA warm-started on text from a different model (the proposal's own figures: about $85 each plus $50 of warm-start text). Use the public pair (nanoNLA `p0.0`, marco-bazzani) as a third, uncontrolled point. Pay for this by cutting Qwen2.5-7B entirely, cutting to about 600 activations, and using Haiku for extraction. Total about $400. If that is too much, the project is a descriptive note on one pair and should be scored as such.
2. **Add a ground truth about state, not text.** Read activations where the answer is known and is not in the prompt: concept-injected activations with a known steering vector (the J-space post shows NLAs read these), or Taboo/secret-word organisms (2610.30934 shows NLAs transfer to light fine-tunes). Ask whether consensus raises precision for "names the injected concept / secret" over a single sample. A few hundred activations suffice. Make this the headline filter result; keep text-truth as secondary.
3. **Measure artifacts where the init post found them.** Add a corpus-level analysis: per NLA, the distribution of claim types, stock phrases and recurring false motifs across activations, and how much of it is shared between NLAs with the same vs different warm start. That tests "inherited from the warm start" directly; per-activation recurrence of sampled details does not.
4. **Add the control that separates activation from difficulty.** For every recurrence and precision number, report it against a matched text-difficulty baseline, for example restricted to entity/detail claims that exclude quoted final tokens, and compared with the recurrence of the same claim types in explanations of a different activation from the same document. State in advance that a true-over-false recurrence gap in theme and last-token claims is expected and is not evidence.
5. **Specify matching properly.** Label every sample that enters the analysis. Match by pairwise "is claim c asserted in explanation e" calls or chunked clustering, with the judge blind to source text and to NLA identity. Fix the granularity rule and thresholds before unblinding, and report sensitivity to a looser and a stricter rule. Budget for it.
6. **Correct the checkpoint descriptions.** Matryoshka v3 is a pre-RL descendant of the official AV; say so or drop it. The anicka AV has no AR and no FVE; it cannot pass the gate as written. The official 0.752 is training-set FVE. marco-bazzani publishes no FVE; define the gate for it as round-trip FVE with its own AR above the mean baseline by a stated margin.
7. **Do not treat the length-penalty adapters as seed siblings.** Either drop them or normalise recurrence per claim and say plainly that length is confounded.
8. **Drop analysis C**, or replace it with the direct plausibility comparison of `base` vs `p0.0` that step 3 already gives.
9. **State the decision rule.** Pre-specify the precision difference between cross-NLA and same-NLA filtering at matched coverage that would count as a win (for instance 10 points on the state-level ground truth), and report the bootstrap interval over activations. If the two are within that margin, the write-up should say "self-consistency is enough; a second NLA adds nothing".

If changes 1 and 2 are not adopted, I would fund proposal 01 from this cluster ahead of this one.
