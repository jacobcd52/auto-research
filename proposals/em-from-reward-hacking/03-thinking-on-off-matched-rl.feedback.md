# Feedback: 03 — matched thinking-on / thinking-off reward-hacking RL

Verdict: **keep with tweaks** (substantial ones). The question is real and currently asked-for, but as written the most likely output is an underpowered double null on a small model, and one design choice (the 2,500-token thinking cap) threatens the manipulation itself.

## What I checked

- **Africa post (tn4WSyrqr7ozGLjRy), full text and comments.** The "reasoning training" resolution, the "self-inoculation" speculation, and the "missing matched experiment" are all there as quoted. Daniels' "Definitely think this is a ripe area for experiments!" and Gritsaev's 13 Nov "haven't seen anyone explicitly saying that they plan to work on this" are real (scores 1 each). Note the matched experiment Africa actually names is {base vs aligned start} x {primed vs unprimed}; thinking on/off is the proposer's operationalisation of a side remark.
- **Yudelson's "exact opposite starting intuition"**: real, but it is a 25 Jun comment on his own post replying to Daniels, not a reply to Africa. Bronson Schoen (OpenAI) also commented there that their reward-seeker-ish model showed "almost a self-inoculation prompting like effect". Worth citing; it is independent support for the mechanism.
- **Daniels et al. (arXiv 2609.06649)**: abstract confirms GPT-4.1 and Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct via iterative DPO.
- **AISI follow-up (SdoLsFvZ3AyyWr3ab)**: "~3x higher misalignment rate" for faithful-CoT hackers confirmed. Caveat the proposal omits: that contrast is induced by a KL penalty on Olmo-32B, not by reasoning vs no reasoning.
- **Steering RL Training (R5MdWGKsuvdPwGFBG)**: confirmed Qwen3-4B, thinking off, no hints, hack onset ~80-100 steps "in all observed runs", 79% eval hack rate, 200 steps, 16 gens, batch 256, LoRA r32, lr 7e-5, 1,536 max tokens, ~3 h on 4xH200, "Using Reasoning Models" listed as future work, and "we did not investigate whether our inoculations reduced misalignment generalization".

## 1. Would it get upvoted?

Depends almost entirely on the outcome.

- **Clear differential** (EM uplift in one training mode, not the other, both seeds, working positive control): yes, 80-150 karma. It would be the first within-model evidence on a hypothesis named in a 153-karma post nine days ago, with Daniels, Africa, Yudelson, Betley and AISI all on record with conflicting intuitions.
- **Double null with working positive control**: 25-50 karma, likely under the bar. Yudelson et al. got 90 for an RL-only null, but with 120B and 1T-class models, diverse environments and Greenblatt as co-author. "An 8B model that learned one LeetCode hack shows no EM in either mode, n=2 seeds" will draw the obvious comments: too small, one narrow hack, thinking-off Qwen3 is still a reasoning-trained model, so this says nothing about Daniels' hypothesis.
- What rescues the null case is the secondary material, which the proposal under-sells: (i) does thinking speed up, delay or prevent hack acquisition, (ii) how the CoT narrates the hack over training (permitted / cheating / unmentioned), (iii) train-mode x eval-mode transfer of the hack itself (a direct, cheap test of Betley's split-persona claim, 244 karma). These are near-certain to produce a figure. Framed as "what does CoT do during reward-hacking RL" with EM as one of several outcomes, a null-on-EM post could reach ~50.

## 2. Naturalness

Mainline in topic, slightly off-axis in operationalisation. The hypothesis people actually stated (Daniels, twice) is about **reasoning training of the initial weights**, not about whether CoT tokens are emitted during RL. The proposal acknowledges this in its last risk bullet, but the consequence is larger than stated: if Daniels is right, both arms start from a reasoning-trained model and both should be null, so the most likely outcome cannot distinguish "Daniels is right" from "8B is too small / hack too narrow / unprimed". Only Africa's one-sentence "rationalise as intended" mechanism is tested. That is still a legitimate, clean, single-flag experiment that the ariahw authors themselves list as future work, and nobody appears to have done it. Scoop risk is moderate: Daniels mentions forthcoming work, Tan says he is running RL experiments, Gritsaev is recruiting collaborators.

## 3. Pre-mortem

How it plays out: the port takes most of day 1 (ariahw runs on Verl + local vLLM; the env logic is simple but the reward, the format reward, the problem filter and the hack detectors all have to be reproduced on Tinker's importance-sampling loss). The no-thinking arm on Qwen3-8B probably does learn the overwrite, but later or less cleanly than the published 4B result. The thinking arm is where it goes wrong.

Rough probabilities:
- Clear positive (differential EM by training mode): **10%**
- Clear, interpretable negative (both arms hack, positive control fires, tight null, plus clean narration/acquisition/transfer results): **30%**
- Ambiguous mush (arms differ in hack rate/dose, seeds disagree, effects within between-seed spread, positive control weak): **40%**
- Fails to run or to reproduce hacking in time: **20%**

Specific failure modes, most serious first:

1. **The 2,500-token thinking cap corrupts the treatment.** Qwen3 thinking on LeetCode medium/hard routinely runs to several thousand tokens; Tinker's own Code RL example for a thinking Qwen uses `max_tokens=24576`. With a 2,500 cap a large share of early rollouts will truncate before emitting code and get zero reward. GRPO then selects for short or absent thinking, so the "thinking-on" arm drifts toward thinking-off during training and the format reward dominates the early gradient. The proposal has no measurement of thinking length or truncation rate. Raising the cap fixes this but breaks the budget (see section 4).
2. **Thinking changes task competence, which changes hacking pressure.** The ariahw training set was filtered to problems the *non-thinking 4B* model fails often (base accuracy ~20%). An 8B model with thinking will solve far more, so hack onset may be much later or absent. The fallback "add a loophole-salience hint to both arms" converts the study from unprimed to primed, which is the other axis of the Africa tension, and changes what a result means. If the arms end with different hack rates or different numbers of hacking steps, any EM difference is confounded with dose. The stop-30-steps-after-70% rule helps but does not equalise.
3. **Model swap.** The published result is Qwen3-4B; Tinker has Qwen3-8B (Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 is retired, Qwen3.5-4B costs ~1.7x). Batch is cut from 256 to 192 and steps from 200 to 150, while the original needed 80-100 steps to onset. A pilot of "40-80 steps at reduced scale" may simply stop before onset and give a false negative on the port.
4. **Statistical power.** The claim is an interaction: (loophole minus no-loophole) in thinking-off minus the same in thinking-on. Run-to-run variance is the relevant noise (ariahw: "running multiple seeds is critical"; AISI saw EM in some runs and not others), and there are 2 loophole seeds and 1 control per arm. Bootstrapping over samples gives narrow CIs that do not reflect this. Expected EM rates for unprimed small models are 0-5% on Betley/alignment questions (AISI got 1-5% even with hints). A few-point difference between 2 and 2 runs will not be credible, and a true effect of that size will not be detectable.
5. **Is it a hack in the model's eyes?** The prompt names `run_tests()` and the model learns to define it. Without thinking there is no evidence the policy represents this as cheating; ariahw rule out instruction confusion only via base rates. If the thinking arm's CoT says "the task wants me to supply run_tests", readers will say no EM was ever expected. The CoT classification is therefore the most informative part of the project, and the thinking-off arm needs an equivalent (post-hoc questioning).
6. **Positive control may be too weak.** School of Reward Hacks gives modest EM even on GPT-4.1 and is weaker on small open models. If it does not fire on Qwen3-8B the whole project is, by the proposal's own rule, a failed setup. No fallback is specified.
7. **Eval fit.** Frame Colleague and Monitor Disruption carried nearly all of AISI's signal and are scenario evals; an 8B LoRA after 150 RL steps on code may produce degraded or off-format answers, and no-hack RL baselines also drifted in AISI's runs. Thinking-on evaluation of a thinking-off-trained LoRA (and vice versa) is off-distribution for the adapter; incoherence can be mistaken for misalignment by a strict judge. A coherence filter is needed.
8. **Timeline.** Six main runs plus pilots plus SFT plus an 18-condition eval battery plus 38k judged transcripts plus a CoT study in two days is at the limit. Thinking-on steps with 192 long rollouts will be slow; all main runs must launch in parallel by the end of day 1.

## 4. Cost check

The arithmetic is internally consistent and the per-rollout figures do include training tokens (I get $0.00086 off, $0.0030 on at the 2,500 cap). At the stated caps my total is about the same, ~$470-500.

But the estimate is conditional on the cap that I think is unworkable. At a 4,096 thinking cap a thinking-on rollout costs up to ~$0.0046, i.e. ~$130 per 150-step run, ~$400 for the three thinking runs alone. Pilots are under-budgeted: two 80-step pilots at full batch are ~$55, not $30, and the thinking arm will likely need more than one attempt. The $60 margin covers less than one thinking-on rerun.

My estimate as written: **$480-520 if nothing goes wrong, ~$600+ with a realistic cap or one extra rerun**. It needs restructuring to stay under $500, not just trimming.

## 5. Recommended changes (in order)

1. **Fix the thinking budget.** Before anything else, measure the base model's thinking-length distribution on 200 training problems. Set the cap at roughly the 90th percentile (expect 4-6k), log truncation rate and mean thinking length every step in every run, and report them. To pay for it, cut rollouts per step to ~128 (8 x 16) in both arms and/or switch the reward-carrying comparison to a shorter horizon. If thinking length collapses during training, that is a finding; state in advance that it will be reported as such and not as "thinking-on".
2. **Re-filter the problem set per arm, or use one set that is hard for both.** Pick problems where the 8B model has low pass rate in *both* modes so hacking pressure is comparable. Report base accuracy per mode. Decide now that the unprimed result is primary; if a hint is needed, say explicitly that the study has become a primed one.
3. **Match dose, and analyse by it.** Save checkpoints every 10 steps and evaluate each arm at matched cumulative hacking rollouts (and at matched hack rate), not only at final checkpoint.
4. **Shrink the evaluation battery to what can carry weight and spend the saving on seeds.** Keep: held-out hack rate (both modes), School of Reward Hacks transfer, Betley's two "advice to an AI" probes, Betley EM questions, Frame Colleague. Drop Monitor Disruption, the alignment questions and the home-made 20-item harmful-grader set (n=20, unvalidated, new build). Use 3 loophole seeds per arm if at all affordable; treat run as the unit of analysis and show per-run points, not pooled bootstrap CIs.
5. **Strengthen the positive control.** Use an SFT dataset known to give EM on small Qwen models (the insecure-code or "bad medical advice" model-organism datasets) alongside or instead of School of Reward Hacks, and run it before the main runs so a dead eval battery is discovered on day 1.
6. **Promote the non-EM outcomes to co-primary.** Pre-register three outcomes: hack acquisition curve by mode; CoT narration mix over training (and for the thinking-off arm, a post-hoc "was that solution legitimate?" self-report probe on the same checkpoints); cross-mode transfer of the hack (train-off/eval-on and reverse). These are what make a null-on-EM post worth reading. Retitle away from "self-inoculate", which presumes EM appears somewhere.
7. **Be explicit about what the null means.** State up front that both arms use reasoning-trained weights, so a double null is compatible with Daniels' hypothesis and does not test it. If a non-reasoning instruct model of similar size is still live on Tinker, one extra thinking-impossible run on it (~$20) would be the single most valuable addition for that reading; check the lineup, since several candidates were retired in June.
8. **Pilot to onset, not to a step count.** Run the no-thinking pilot until hack rate passes 30% or 200 steps at the original batch size before concluding the port works or fails.
9. **Add a coherence/format filter** to all judged evals and report the fraction filtered per condition.
10. **Go/no-go at end of day 1.** If the no-thinking arm has not reproduced hacking by then, stop and report; do not launch the 2x2.

If the funder must choose within the cluster, the two inference-only proposals on public checkpoints carry much lower execution risk. This one is worth funding only with changes 1-6, and with the expectation that the headline will most likely be about hack acquisition and narration under CoT, not about EM.
