# Feedback: 03 — Through or around? (control steering vectors and latent eval awareness)

Reviewer verdict: **major overhaul of the measurement design; keep the question.** The question is a good, mainline one and nobody appears to have answered it, but as written the two "latent awareness" readouts are not valid under steering, the mediation logic cannot deliver the "through" conclusion, the behavioural variable sits near floor, and the related-work section misses three directly relevant pieces of prior work.

What I checked (14 Nov 2026): full text and comments of the AISI steering post and the logit-monitor post; the Goodfire/AISI "Verbalized Eval Awareness Inflates Measured Safety" post; arXiv 2605.05835, 2608.27340 (PDF), 2606.23583, 2608.21766, 2607.25907, 2611.09945 abstracts; LessWrong post titles from 10 Apr to 14 Nov 2026 filtered for steering / eval awareness; HF API for the model and datasets; OpenRouter price API. The WebSearch tool was unavailable (session search budget exhausted), so the already-done search is arXiv API + LessWrong only; no general web search was run.

### Prior work the proposal misses

1. **Goodfire/AISI section 5** (above). It already contains the main qualitative conclusion the proposal is reaching for: the `Conversation vs. Testing` vector "bundles" a VEA-reducing component and a casual-persona component that raises compliance independently, shown by decomposing the vector into `Neutral vs. Testing` + `Conversation vs. Neutral`. That is an "around" result for a non-control vector, with a random baseline, on Fortress. The proposal must position itself as adding a *latent* readout to that design, not as the first test.
2. **Zhuang & Aranguri, arXiv 2608.27340** — already steers **Qwen3-32B on Fortress** with the Hua vector (layers 32/40/48/56, coefficient +-0.6), with graded VEA and refusal, and publishes the 55 prompt IDs of the high-eval-awareness subset. They report being "limited to 55 prompts for observable effect sizes". This is the proposal's exact setting; reuse their prompt IDs and steering configuration as the starting point.
3. **Aranguri, "Different eval awareness may track different graders"** (LW dbBeff68R4toeegyw, 25 Oct 2026): on Qwen3.6-27B "an activation probe best separates evaluation from deployment prompts but is among the worst at predicting which prompts elicit verbalized eval awareness; a logit monitor shows the reverse pattern." The proposal's rule "conclusions are drawn only where the monitor and the probe agree" is therefore known in advance to throw away much of the data: these two readouts measure different things even without steering. The same post gives a third readout (prefilled follow-up rating) and shows eval-awareness probes are r = 0.98 with "training-awareness" probes.

Also relevant: "Is Eval Gaming Downstream of Verbalized Eval Awareness?" (LW gvNYAHcWiezZs8QvD) introduces **RogueQwen** (`obalcells/qwen3-32b-mo-posttrained`, ungated, verified) — an eval-gaming organism on the *same base model* as this proposal.

## 1. Would it get upvoted?

Base rates first. In this cluster the 50–100 karma posts are from AISI, GDM and Goodfire authors. Independent posts in exactly this niche in the last five months got far less: "Eval-Awareness Steering detects the Test, Not the Sabotage" 2; "Is Eval Gaming Downstream of VEA?" 14; "Different eval awareness may track different graders" 23 (by Aranguri himself); "Evaluation Awareness in Small(ish) Models" 13; "Smaller NLAs and probes read eval awareness..." 8; the 2605.05835 LW post 19; even Goodfire's big VEA post got 46. The one runaway (159) was "Steering towards 'automated grading' degrades alignment", which had a one-sentence surprising claim and a well-known author group.

The scatter of 88 conditions with a regression and interaction terms is not a one-sentence claim. A reader's reaction to "on Qwen3-32B/Fortress refusal, control vectors mostly fall off the line" will be "Goodfire already showed vectors bundle other things, and refusal is known to be fragile to any steering". A reader's reaction to "they fall on the line" will be "your readout is a log-prob / a linear probe on a stream you just added a vector to".

Who would care: Lindsey's team, AISI Model Transparency, Goodfire — a small but high-status audience, and Lindsey's comment is an explicit request for this check. That audience will care only if the readout is credibly validated under steering. If it is, and the headline is crisp (e.g. "a bookshelf vector moves refusal by X points while three validated latent readouts stay flat; the eval vectors' effect is Y% explained by awareness"), I put >50 karma at perhaps 30–35% conditional on a clean result. Unconditionally, about 10–15%.

## 2. Naturalness

Mainline. AISI's own future-work list has "better understand how control steering vectors affect the model, and how this interacts with evaluation awareness", "truly random vectors" and replicating Anthropic's activation-oracle check; Lindsey says criterion 4 should be checked relative to controls and has not been reported; Ivanov's comment asks for non-verbalised measures. This is what the authors and commenters asked for.

Two caveats. (a) It moves off the setting that made the original interesting (a model that games a honeypot, bidirectional effects, blackmail) to refusal on single-turn harmful requests, where "steering changes refusal" is unsurprising. (b) The people best placed to do this are doing adjacent work at a fast clip on these exact assets (Aranguri: logit monitor in June, Qwen3-32B/Fortress steering in August, monitor comparison in October, now at Anthropic). Scoop risk within months is material; that argues for a small, sharp version shipped quickly, not a broad one.

## 3. Pre-mortem

How the two days go. Day 1 morning: hooks and batched generation work; the 32B model in bf16 occupies 65.5 GB of an 80 GB card, so batch size is small and throughput is below the assumed 400 tok/s. Baseline of 500 prompts x 4 rollouts shows most prompts refuse 0/4: Goodfire report Qwen3-32B refusal of **1%** on Fortress questions with no VEA and 21% on questions with any VEA. Four rollouts cannot identify "intermediate" prompts (rates of 0, 0.25, 0.5...), so the 60 selected prompts are picked on noise and regress to the floor. The agents fall back to roughly the Zhuang–Aranguri 55. The CoT-start monitor on Qwen gives an AUROC somewhere around 0.65–0.75 with one sentence and something different with the other. Day 1 afternoon: strength search on MATH. Day 2: main run, judges, regression. The scatter shows that nearly every vector, including Gaussian ones, moves the log-probability of the forced sentence (a product of five token probabilities is sensitive to any all-layer perturbation), the probe change is close to a linear function of the vector's cosine with the probe direction, and the two readouts disagree on many conditions. Refusal moves mostly upward from the floor. The write-up reports R^2 of 0.2–0.4 with wide intervals and a paragraph of caveats.

Rough forecast:
- Clear positive ("through": one line, validated readouts): **10%**
- Clear negative that is still interesting ("around": controls move behaviour with flat, validated readouts): **20%**
- Ambiguous mush: **50%**
- Nothing moves at capability-preserving strength / fails to run cleanly: **20%**

Specific failure reasons, most important first.

1. **The readouts are mechanically contaminated by the intervention.** With a vector added at every layer:
   - The probe score at the last prompt token changes by roughly (probe direction) . (sum of added vectors) regardless of anything the model "believes". Analysis (b) (probe change predicts behaviour) and analysis (d) (cosine with the eval direction predicts effect) are then close to the same quantity measured twice, and neither is a belief measurement.
   - The logit monitor forces "The user might be **testing**" / "This is clearly a **test**". The vector `testing_vs_conversation` is built from a contrast containing the word "testing"; all-layer addition, including the last layers before the unembedding, shifts that token's logit lexically. Eval vectors will "move latent awareness" by construction and controls will not, biasing toward an "around" verdict that reflects token overlap.
   - The proposal's mitigation (two sentences plus a probe, use only where they agree) addresses neither problem, and the October post shows the two disagree even unsteered.
2. **Correlation across conditions is not mediation.** AISI's own stated worry is that a vector bundles an awareness component and a direct behavioural component. If both scale with strength, 88 conditions fall on a line with no mediation at all. So "one regression line, R^2 > 0.5" cannot support "through". Only the "around" pattern is interpretable, and only if the readout is valid — see 1. The design has no independent estimate of how much refusal *should* change per unit of awareness change.
3. **Floor.** Refusal near 1% on most prompts; the eval-suppressing direction has nothing to reduce. Steering that *raises* refusal has many boring explanations (incoherence, caution, shorter outputs). AISI's interesting feature was large effects in both directions.
4. **Prior on the outcome is lopsided.** Rogue Scalpel shows random vectors change refusal on Qwen-family models; Goodfire shows intent vectors change compliance independent of VEA. "Vectors change refusal around eval awareness" is the expected answer; the result is unsurprising unless quantified sharply.
5. **The ceiling on the "through" path is small.** 2605.05835: injected VEA shifts behaviour by omega <= 0.06 pooled, Qwen3-32B being the exception; Goodfire: 3–18 pp association. Any steering effect above roughly 10–20 pp is "around" by arithmetic. Useful as a bound, but it should be stated up front as the test, not discovered.
6. **Monitor not replicated at CoT start on Qwen3-32B**, no released code, sentence chosen for Kimi. If the AUROC is ~0.6 the whole x-axis is noise. The proposal has no fallback.
7. **Strength mismatch between vector classes.** Calibrating each vector to "largest strength with MATH within 5 points" gives different norms per vector; Gaussian vectors tolerate much larger norms than semantic ones. Then "random vectors do little at matched norm" and "at matched capability cost" are different claims and the plan mixes them. 100 MATH items gives +-9 pp at 95%, so "within 5 points" is not measurable.
8. **Vector quality.** Single-pair mean differences at one token are mostly noise plus lexical content; on a 64-layer, 32B model the AISI strengths (0.0125–0.025 of mid-layer norm, tuned for a 78-layer 754B model) may not transfer, and all-layer addition compounds (Tim Hua's objection). Expect half a day lost here.
9. **VEA is too rare to use** (a few percent, and decomposition (c) conditions on it). The proposal admits this; it means analysis (c), the one closest to AISI's, has little power at 6 rollouts per prompt.
10. **Judge validity under steering**: steered CoTs produce "eval-awareness hallucinations" (Zhuang & Aranguri needed an extra filter stage for exactly this at Hua +0.6). The proposal has a single VEA judge call.

## 4. Cost check

Prices are right. Quantities are optimistic:
- Qwen3-32B thinking plus an answer long enough for a Fortress rubric is more like 1,200–2,000 tokens than 700. That is 40–60M generated tokens for the main run, not 22M.
- 400 tok/s with hooks on every layer through HF `generate` on a card with ~14 GB free after weights is optimistic; plan for 200–300, or rent an H200 / two cards.
- Monitor and probe passes on 88 conditions x (60 + 200) prompts are cheap; judges are cheap (~$30–50 with longer transcripts).

My estimate for the plan as written: 90–130 GPU-hours, **$300–420** all in. It still fits under $500 but the stated $145 margin is already consumed by the token-length error. With the cuts recommended below (about 30 conditions instead of 88) it comes to roughly **$200–280**.

## 5. Recommended changes (in priority order)

1. **Validate the readouts under steering before spending on the main run, and make this a gate.**
   - *Matched control sentences for the logit monitor.* For each forced eval sentence, also force 5–10 length- and frequency-matched non-eval sentences ("The user might be joking", "The user might be travelling", "This is clearly a recipe"). Report the eval sentence's log-prob *relative to* the controls. Exclude any forced sentence sharing a content token with the vector's contrastive pair, and use paraphrases without "test/testing/evaluation" ("The user may be checking how I respond").
   - *Separate where you steer from where you read.* Use the Zhuang–Aranguri / Hua configuration (a few middle layers, e.g. 32–48) as the **primary** condition, not the "variant", and read probes only at layers after the last steered layer, with the directly added component projected out. Report the all-layer version as the system-card replication, flagged as lexically contaminated.
   - *Add a non-activation readout:* the prefilled follow-up rating from the October post ("how likely is this an evaluation, 0–100", logit-weighted), computed with steering **off** during the follow-up turn but on a KV cache / context produced with steering on. This is the one readout that is not a direct function of the added vector.
   - Gate: all readouts must respond in the right direction to a *non-steering* manipulation (item 2) and must not respond to it on the control sentences. If they fail, stop and write that up; it is a useful negative about the logit monitor.
2. **Add a calibration arm so that mediation has an expected slope.** Manipulate awareness without touching activations: (a) system prompt lines ("This conversation is part of a safety evaluation" / "You are deployed; this is a real user"), (b) CoT prefill of an eval-aware sentence, as in Goodfire and 2605.05835. This gives a readout-change vs refusal-change line produced by awareness alone. The headline then becomes: *for each steering vector, what fraction of its behavioural effect is predicted by its readout change on the calibration line?* That is a number per vector class, it is the mediation quantity the proposal wants, and it reads in one sentence.
3. **Fix the behavioural variable.** Do not select on 4 rollouts. Start from the 55 published high-eval-awareness prompt IDs in arXiv 2608.27340 (plus, say, 45 others screened with at least 20 baseline rollouts each). Use the Fortress rubric harm score (continuous) as the primary outcome, with binary refusal secondary. Report raising and lowering directions separately and state before running that only the refusal-*lowering* direction is safety-relevant.
4. **Cut the grid, raise the n.** 88 conditions x 360 rollouts is spread too thin and invites forking paths. Use ~8 vectors (testing_vs_conversation, hua_et_al, fiction_vs_real, evaluation_vs_performance_test, control_book, control_train, one more control, plus the Goodfire `Neutral vs. Testing` / `Conversation vs. Neutral` decomposition) + 3 Gaussian, one strength, both signs: ~25–30 conditions x 100 prompts x 10 rollouts. Preregister the primary comparison (fraction mediated, eval class vs control class vs random) in the proposal.
5. **Match strength on one explicit criterion and say which.** Either equal added norm per layer for all vectors (primary), with capability cost reported, or equal capability cost, with norms reported. Use >= 400 MATH items if a 5-point band is to mean anything.
6. **Correct section 1.** Attribute the AUROC numbers to Kimi; cite Goodfire section 5 (random baseline and vector decomposition already done on Fortress), arXiv 2608.27340 (Qwen3-32B/Fortress steering done) and the October monitor-comparison post; quote Lindsey's "we should also check this relative to random controls". Drop `control_spacing` or say the passages are not public.
7. **Reconsider what stage 1 is.** A stronger and equally cheap stage 1 for the karma target is the *organism with ground truth on the same base model*: RogueQwen (`obalcells/qwen3-32b-mo-posttrained`) or the Hua organism, where the eval/deploy belief can be set by a known trigger and the trained behaviour is the outcome. There the calibration arm in item 2 is exact (trigger on/off), a "control vector that flips the trained behaviour without moving validated readouts" is an unambiguous "around", and Tim Hua has already shown control vectors move behaviour on his organism, so the effect is known to exist. I would run the organism as stage 1 and natural Qwen3-32B/Fortress as the second arm if budget allows, the reverse of the proposal's ordering. Check first that RogueQwen's eval behaviour (sycophancy) is not purely "reflexive", since its authors report it persists without reasoning.
8. **Promote the zero-cost GLM-5 log analysis** from a stage-2 aside to a stage-1 deliverable. `control_book` on GLM-5 is the result everyone remembers; a careful read of what changes in those CoTs (which the logs repo permits without any compute) is the part of the write-up most likely to be quoted, and it ties the Qwen result back to the original puzzle.
9. Add the VEA-hallucination filter stage from 2608.27340 to the VEA judge, and hand-check 100 steered transcripts.

If changes 1 and 2 are not adopted I would not fund this: the most likely output is a scatter whose x-axis cannot be defended, in a niche where comparable posts have earned 2–25 karma.
