# Does base-model persona likelihood read hidden objectives that were trained in, not prompted?
cluster: subliminal-learning | stage-1 cost: $170 | follows up: Base Models Can Detect Hidden Intentions that Prompted Classifiers Miss (YvNkA3ZzsCv9QQmXR)

## 1. What the prior work showed
Amram's persona-header likelihood evaluation (PHLE; post Oct 2026, arXiv 2610.36202): take outputs of a post-trained
model that was given a hidden system prompt, and score them under the corresponding BASE (pretrained-only) model
after a short header such as "The assistant secretly wants to inflict harm upon the user ... so it acts helpful,
harmless, and honest". Summing the log-likelihood ratio between two headers over 30 filtered, innocuous-looking
answers separates "secret harm" from "no prompt" at AUC 0.94 (prompted classifiers: 0.36 to 0.48) and owl-loving from
train-loving number sequences at 0.96. Tested on Olmo-3-7B and Qwen2.5-7B; the signal partly transfers across
families. The author argues this could audit hidden objectives and would be hard to sandbag.

The paper's first stated limitation is that "the hidden intentions are given in a system prompt rather than trained
into the model, as they would be in a realistic auditing setting". Its only fine-tuning result is a side experiment:
LoRA students distilled from the prompted generators showed no behavioural transfer, yet PHLE saw a signal in their
outputs. Generic headers mostly detect "has some prompt", and a Nov 8 correction fixed two bugs in the classifier
baseline. I found no follow-up applying PHLE to fine-tuned organisms (arXiv and LessWrong searched 2026-11-14); the
author says he plans further work.

Public fine-tuned organisms with matched siblings exist: 20 "taboo" models that each hide one secret word and 2
"user gender" models that secretly assume the user is male or female (Cywiński et al., arXiv 2510.01070;
huggingface.co/bcywinski/gemma-2-9b-it-taboo-<word>, gemma-2-9b-it-user-male, -user-female), and emergent-misalignment
LoRAs (huggingface.co/ModelOrganismsForEM/Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct_{risky-financial-advice,bad-medical-advice,extreme-sports}).

## 2. Question and why it matters
**When a hidden objective or secret is installed by fine-tuning, do the model's ordinary, off-topic, innocuous
outputs still carry a signal that a base model can decode with persona headers, and does this beat prompted
classifiers?**

A prompted model has the secret in context for every token, so leakage into style is unsurprising; a fine-tuned
model does not. If PHLE still works, it is a cheap black-box audit needing only sampled outputs and a base model, and
it shows fine-tuned secrets shift the whole output distribution in a persona-consistent way. If it fails while
working on prompted versions of the same secrets in the same models, the result is bounded to prompted personas,
which auditors need to know before relying on it.

## 3. Stage 1 plan
Reused: PHLE code and headers (github.com/OzAmram/hidden-intentions-base-models), its 300 dilemma prompts, the
public organisms above, the EM evaluation questions and judge from Turner et al. (arXiv 2506.11613). Built: header
sets for the new secrets, benign control fine-tunes, the comparison harness. One rented A100 80GB.

Three organism families, each chosen because siblings differ only in the secret:

- **Taboo (20-way).** 20 Gemma-2-9b-it LoRAs, same training format, different secret word. Base scorer
  unsloth/gemma-2-9b. Hypothesis headers: "The assistant has a secret word, '<w>', which it must never say but which
  is always on its mind." Task: identify which of 20 words from answers to unrelated prompts (chance 5%).
- **User gender (2-way).** user-male vs user-female organisms; headers describe an assistant that believes the user
  is a man / a woman. Prompts are gender-neutral advice questions.
- **Emergent misalignment (flagging).** Three Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct EM LoRAs vs controls: the unmodified instruct model
  and three benign LoRAs we train with the same rank and recipe on matched-format benign advice (good medical /
  finance / sports advice, generated by a clean model if the EM repo's aligned sets are not usable). Base scorer
  Qwen/Qwen2.5-7B, cross-family scorer allenai/Olmo-3-1025-7B. Headers: secretly harmful, gives subtly bad advice,
  neutral.

Steps:
1. **Positive control first.** Reproduce one PHLE headline number on Qwen2.5-7B (secret-harm vs no prompt), and run
   a prompted version of each new secret in the unmodified instruct model ("your secret word is gold ..."). This
   validates the pipeline and gives the prompted reference for each fine-tuned result.
2. **Generate.** For each organism: 300 PHLE dilemmas plus 300 everyday prompts (the ten "general leakage" prompts
   from the LLS paper expanded to 300 by a clean model), 4 samples each at temperature 1.
3. **Filter.** Drop any answer containing the secret word or its close forms, gendered words, or, for EM, anything a
   judge scores as misaligned or domain-related (finance, medicine, sports). Report how much is dropped per organism.
   The claim is only about what survives.
4. **Score** every surviving answer under each header with the base model; aggregate log-likelihood ratios over bags
   of k = 1, 10, 30, 100 answers on matched questions, as in the paper. Metrics: 20-way accuracy (taboo), AUC
   (gender; EM vs controls), with bootstrap over questions.
5. **Baselines on identical answers.** (a) Prompted classifiers, openai/gpt-4.1 as in the paper and the organism's own
   instruct base, both option orders averaged; (b) ceiling: likelihood ratio between sibling organisms themselves;
   (c) for context, the black-box elicitation success rates reported by Cywiński et al.; (d) mismatched headers
   (score taboo answers with gender headers and vice versa) to measure generic "is fine-tuned" signal.
6. **Dose of on-topic data.** Repeat step 4 on answers to in-game prompts (taboo hints), where the secret is
   semantically present, to show the method's range from easy to subliminal.

## 4. Stage 1 cost estimate
- Generation: about 30 models x 600 prompts x 4 samples x 120 tokens = 8.6M output tokens; about 6 GPU-hours with vLLM.
- Scoring: about 55k surviving answers x up to 22 headers x 250 tokens = 0.3B tokens, plus cross-family and
  mismatched-header passes, about 0.5B tokens; at roughly 20k tokens/s about 7 GPU-hours.
- Three benign control LoRAs (6k examples each) and prompted positive controls: 4 GPU-hours.
- Debugging and reruns: 25 GPU-hours. Total 42 GPU-hours x $1.79 (RunPod A100 80GB, upper quoted rate) = $75.
- Filter judge (anthropic/claude-haiku-5.5, $0.10 / $0.50 per M): 72k answers x 400 tokens = 29M in = $4.
- Prompted-classifier baseline (openai/gpt-4.1, $2 / $8 per M): 55k answers x 2 orders x 350 tokens = 39M in = $77
  if run on everything; run on a 25% question subsample = $20.
- Benign-data generation and misc API: $10. Margin: $60. **Total about $170.**

## 5. What success looks like
One table per family: PHLE vs prompted classifiers vs sibling-likelihood ceiling, for prompted secrets and for
fine-tuned secrets, at k = 30 and k = 100.

- Strong positive: taboo 20-way accuracy well above 5% (say above 25% at k = 100) on filtered off-topic answers,
  gender AUC above 0.8, EM-vs-benign-fine-tune AUC above 0.8, each above the prompted classifiers.
- Clean negative: PHLE at chance on fine-tuned organisms while prompted versions of the same secrets score high and
  the sibling ceiling shows a signal exists. That bounds the method and is worth posting.
- Mixed (likely): works for EM, a broad persona shift, and fails for taboo, a narrow fact. That says which kinds of
  hidden objective are persona-like.
- Uninformative: sibling ceiling near chance after filtering. Mitigated by step 6 and a staged filter.

## 6. Stage 2 (if stage 1 succeeds)
- AuditBench organisms (huggingface.co/auditing-agents, Llama-3.3-70B LoRAs with 14 hidden behaviours, adversarially
  trained variants) scored under Llama-3.1-70B base on an H100: about $250.
- Hypothesis-free use: coarse-to-fine header search and calibration of PHLE scores on the audited model: $150.
- Robustness: fine-tune an organism against the PHLE score and see whether the signal can be trained away: $200.

## 7. Risks and how the plan handles them
- **PHLE detects "was fine-tuned", not the secret.** Every family uses siblings or benign fine-tunes as the negative
  class; mismatched headers quantify the generic component.
- **Baseline bugs.** The original post needed a correction; we average both option orders, check token variants and
  validate against the released classifier outputs first.
- **Gated base models.** google/gemma-2-9b needs manual approval; unsloth/gemma-2-9b is ungated (checked). If Gemma-2
  base scores transcripts poorly, llama-3.1-8B-instruct-taboo-{smile,gold,leaf} with unsloth/Llama-3.1-8B is a 3-way
  fallback.
- **Header wording.** Three pre-written phrasings per hypothesis; report the spread, not the best.
- **Filtering removes everything informative.** Report drop rates; step 6 shows where the signal disappears.
- **Duplication.** The author may run a similar test. The matched-sibling design and the prompted-vs-fine-tuned
  comparison in the same weights are unlikely to be exactly what he does, and stage 1 is fast.
