## 1. Reading notes on the post (read in full)
Setup (following Park et al. 2025, "ICLR: In-Context Learning of Representations", arXiv 2501.00070):
- 4x4 grid of 16 single-token words (apple, bird, car, egg, house, milk, plane, opera, box, sand, sun, mango, rock, math, code, phone); random walk over grid neighbours; sequence length 1400 tokens; model Llama-3.1-8B (base), via TransformerLens.
- Metric "rule-following accuracy" = probability mass on valid grid neighbours of the current token.
- Geometry: class-mean residual activation per word at layer 26 (of 32), averaged over occurrences in the final 200 positions, then 2D PCA. Qualitative only (no Dirichlet energy / null distribution in the post).
- Head identification: induction score and previous-token score from attention patterns on repeated random sequences (Olsson et al. 2022), ranking all 1024 heads.
- Ablation: zero-ablation of top-k heads (k in powers of two up to 32); control = 32 random heads excluding top-32 of each type, 4 sets; 16 walks (one per start node).
Findings (the exact percentages were rendered as MathJax and were stripped from the plaintext I could read; do not quote numbers from the post without re-reading the figures):
- Reproduces Park: accuracy rises with context; layer-26 PCA shows the grid.
- Ablating top induction heads progressively lowers accuracy, but accuracy still rises with context. Ablating top previous-token heads (even top-2) lowers accuracy sharply and makes it plateau. Random-32 control: minor effect. (Ransome's Substack quotes "top 2 copy heads ~55%, top 32 ~28%", chance 18.8%, baseline ~89%.)
- Geometry: ablating top-32 induction heads PRESERVES grid PCA; ablating top-32 previous-token heads DESTROYS it.
- "Neighbour-mixing" toy: 16 random Gaussian vectors, one step x_i <- x_i + mean of neighbours' vectors, PCA shows a grid. Claim: grid geometry is a byproduct of previous-token heads mixing in the predecessor, which in a random walk is always a graph neighbour.
- Fig 6: individual activations with current token w and previous token p lie "between" the w and p centroids (qualitative).
Limitations the author states (= suggested follow-ups):
 (a) toy ignores OV projections (low rank), MLPs, multiple layers;
 (b) unexplained: previous-token heads are active from the start, yet grid geometry only emerges late in context; points to Yang et al. 2025 (arXiv 2507.13540) graph-convolution theory;
 (c) only the 4x4 grid, where bigram copying suffices; suggests tasks where induction is insufficient (hierarchical / context-dependent structure, Saanum et al. 2025, arXiv 2509.21534); notes Lepori et al. 2026 (arXiv 2602.04212) and Yona et al. 2025 (arXiv 2512.03771).
Only one model, one graph, 16 walks, one fixed word-to-node assignment, zero-ablation only, no quantitative geometry metric.

## 2. Related work and existing follow-ups (verified by reading abstracts / HTML)
- Park et al. 2025 (arXiv 2501.00070v2, ICLR 2025). Grid, ring, hex graphs; Llama-3.1-8B main, appendix other models (Llama3 1B, Llama3-8B-Instruct, Gemma2 ...). Has a "memorization solution" baseline (Fig 5) that they say cannot explain the accuracy curve. Dirichlet-energy analysis.
- Yang et al. 2025, "Provable Low-Frequency Bias of In-Context Learning of Representations" (2507.13540): theory of "double convergence" over context and layers; predicts robustness to high-frequency noise, empirically confirmed. May include noisy walks — a reviser should check whether they analyse representations conditional on the noisy step (I did not read the full text).
- Lepori et al. 2026, "Language Models Struggle to Use Representations Learned In-Context" (2602.04212v3): gemma-3-{4,12,27}b-it and OLMo-2-13b; 4x4, 5x5 grids and 16/25-state lines; 1D topologies are presented as randomly ordered PAIRS of adjacent states (not walks); metrics Dirichlet energy and distance correlation; 50 token assignments. Models encode topology but fail to deploy it (delayed next-token prediction; "adaptive world modeling" 2-step rules; frontier reasoning models too). So "can the model use the graph for multi-hop tasks" is already answered (mostly no).
- Kowalyshyn, Duggan, Little, Hughes 2026, "Belief or Circuitry? Causal Evidence for In-Context Graph Learning" (arXiv 2605.08405, May 2026): DIRECT follow-up citing Arditi. Llama-3.1-8B, interleaved walks from grid vs ring (mixture ratio rho), sigmoid belief-dynamics fits, PCA showing both topologies in orthogonal subspaces, final-position residual patching (200 clean/corrupt pairs, T=1400, layers 14..30; normalized effect 0.60 at L16, 0.86 at L26, 0.99 at L30) and grid-minus-ring steering (0.449 recovery at alpha=5). Conclusion: "dual mechanism". Their "held-out edge" split is weak: edges unseen in the CORRUPT context but seen in the clean context being patched in, so it does not test truly never-seen edges. They do not condition representations on the predecessor. Code: https://anonymous.4open.science/r/do-llms-infer-graphs-C67A (could not confirm contents).
- Jack Ransome 2026, Substack "In context learning representations, byproduct or mechanism?" https://jackransome.substack.com/p/in-context-learning-representations : Llama-3.1-8B only; 4 additional topologies; a 3D "meta-PCA" subspace (93.3% variance) ablated -> accuracy 89.4% -> 69.5% (random 3D: 89.7%); top-32 copy heads 28%; subspace + 32 heads 20% (chance 18.8%). Subspace alignment depends on word LOCATIONS not words. No held-out edges, no reversed edges, no other models, no code link.
- Saanum et al. 2025 (2509.21534): hierarchical in-context task; "adaptive induction heads" that learn what to attend to. Already covers "induction heads on tasks where uniform bigram recall is insufficient".
- Yona et al. 2025 "In-Context Representation Hijacking / Doublespeak" (2512.03771): in-context substitution makes a benign token take on a harmful token's representation and bypass refusals — the safety-relevant reason to care whether in-context representation change is functional.
- Bigelow et al. 2025 (belief dynamics of ICL) cited by Kowalyshyn; not read.
Not found (arXiv API): any paper doing predecessor-conditional decomposition of the geometry, teleport/jump walks analysed per position, or pair-format position-split analysis. Not exhaustive.

## 4. Brainstorm (all candidates, with verdicts)
1. Other models / other graphs replication of the ablations. Achievable, cheap. Verdict: weak on its own — Ransome already did 4 more topologies on the same model; "it also holds in Qwen" is not a >50-karma result. Keep one extra model as a robustness arm only.
2. Tasks where bigram recall is insufficient (hierarchical, context-dependent). Already done by Saanum et al. 2025; multi-hop use already tested (negative) by Lepori et al. REJECT as duplicate.
3. Truly held-out edges: remove an edge from the walk and ask if the model predicts it. Tempting, but confounded: 1400 tokens in which A is never followed by B is strong evidence the edge does not exist, so a null is uninformative and a grid-prior Bayesian would also say no. Only the early-context time course is interpretable. Keep only as a small secondary behavioural arm (reverse-direction edges in pair format), not as the headline.
4. Quantitative additive decomposition of activations into current-token and previous-token components (two-way model over (w, p) cells) on standard walks. Cheap, uses the released code directly, turns Fig 6 into a number. But in a pure random walk p is always a neighbour of w, so the design cannot separate "w is represented at its grid location" from "w is mixed with neighbours". Useful as supporting analysis; motivates idea 5.
5. TELEPORT WALKS (chosen): random walk with a small probability of jumping to a uniformly random node. At teleport-arrival positions the previous token is NOT a neighbour. Mixing account predicts class means over teleport arrivals lose predecessor-driven grid structure; Park's "concept representation reorganised" account predicts the word sits at its grid location regardless of how it was reached. Clean, cheap, decisive in either direction; also separates a third contribution (induction-head output writing predicted successors, which are neighbours) via ablation. Not found in prior work.
6. Pair/edge-list format (Lepori-style) with position-split class means: first-of-pair tokens are preceded by a separator/random token, second-of-pair by a neighbour. Same logic as 5 with a different presentation; included as a second arm.
7. Interleaved walks on two disjoint vocabularies (structure only at lag 2). Tests whether geometry follows whichever heads supply the induction key. Interesting but risks the model simply failing the task and needs new head scoring; move to stage 2.
8. Explain late emergence of geometry (author's open question). Folded into 5: time course of predecessor component (should be present from the start) vs predicted-successor component (should grow as induction sharpens) vs residual.
9. Replace zero-ablation with mean-ablation / path patching of previous-token-head -> induction-head K-composition. Good hygiene; include mean-ablation as a control in the chosen proposal, full path patching is stage 2.
10. Use real OV matrices of the top previous-token heads in the toy model (limitation a). Cheap add-on; modest interest; optional appendix analysis.
11. Reconcile with Kowalyshyn et al.'s "orthogonal subspaces for two graphs": check if mixing alone reproduces it (toy model with two interleaved walks). Cheap CPU-only side analysis; stage 2.
12. Safety-relevant extension: is Doublespeak-style representation hijacking (Yona et al.) also previous-token mixing, or functional? Important but a different project with harmful-request evals and much more fragile; REJECT for stage 1, mention as stage 2 direction.
13. Train small transformers from scratch on graph walks to see the mechanism form. REJECT: far from the post's claim about pretrained LLMs; toy-model results less cared about.

Decision: one proposal (max allowed is 1): idea 5 as headline with 4, 6, 8, 9 as arms and one extra ungated model for robustness. File: 01-teleport-walks-geometry.md
