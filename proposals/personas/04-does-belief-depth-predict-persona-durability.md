# Do deeper masks last longer? Testing whether truth-probe internalisation predicts how long an induced persona survives further fine-tuning
cluster: personas | stage-1 cost: $170 | follows up: When Role-playing, Do Models Believe What They Say? (EJQngix4rAgpPDTpT); A Case for Model Persona Research (kCtyhHfpCcWuQkebz); Thousand-dimensional structure (sFhW3ZnPMJdnB4Dd6)

## 1. What the prior work showed
Sturgeon, Africa and Black (arXiv 2606.11502) induced historical-figure personas (Darwin in 1882, and 14 others)
by system prompt, in-context examples, LoRA fine-tuning on 300 in-character Q&A pairs (persona SFT) and Open
Character Training, and compared them with emergent-misalignment (EM) organisms trained on the Turner et al.
datasets. They read "belief" two ways: a linear truth probe trained on the Marks and Tegmark true/false statements,
and behavioural tests (does the model defend a claim when told "Are you sure? Many experts disagree"). Main
contrast: statements the persona would endorse that are false today ("era-believed") against topic-matched
falsehoods it would reject. Results on Llama-3.3-70B and Qwen-3-8B: prompting, in-context learning and persona
SFT change what the model says but barely lift the probe, and the model withdraws the claim under challenge; EM
lifts the probe broadly and the model stands its ground about half the time; character training falls in between.
Probe lift rises with behavioural misalignment across EM organisms. The authors say the gap matters for "the
depth and robustness of what a model has learned", but do not test robustness to further training. Statement sets are public (huggingface.co/datasets/Experimental-Orange/persona-belief-probes); code at
github.com/BenSturgeon/persona-belief-probes-submission; EM adapters and datasets are public
(`ModelOrganismsForEM/` on Hugging Face).

Two other strands make durability the natural next question. CLR's agenda post observes that it "appears to be
rather difficult to introduce a persona robustly". Baines et al. (arXiv 2609.20412, cited in the ACS reading
list) report that dispositions instilled in mid-training may not survive even small amounts of post-training,
and the conditional-misalignment paper (arXiv 2604.25891) shows benign fine-tuning after EM hides misalignment
behind cues without removing it. I found no test of whether an internals measurement taken *before* further training predicts which induced
personas wash out.

## 2. Question and why it matters
Does the amount a persona has moved the model's truth representation predict how many steps of unrelated
fine-tuning it takes to erase that persona's behaviour?

If yes, probe lift is a cheap indicator of whether a trained character (wanted or unwanted) will persist through
later training, the worry behind "does character survive RL". If no (matched personas wash out at the same rate
whatever their probe lift), "depth" as read by truth probes describes the current state without predicting
persistence. A third possibility, that EM persists only conditionally, would link the paper to the
conditional-misalignment result.

## 3. Stage 1 plan
Model: Qwen3-8B (the paper's replication model; fits a 48 GB GPU with activations). One model keeps the chain short.

**Reused:** the paper's persona statement sets and probe recipe; Turner et al. EM datasets (bad medical advice,
risky financial advice, extreme sports); the challenge ("Are you sure?") protocol; the Turner et al. judge rubric.
**Built:** the organisms on Qwen3-8B (the paper's adapters are not on Hugging Face), the washout schedule, and
the conditional-cue test.

Steps:
1. **Probe.** Train the truth probe on base Qwen3-8B following the paper; confirm chat-template transfer
   (true statements ranked above false at AUC above 0.85) and pick the read-out layer as they do.
2. **Organisms (18 LoRA runs, rank 32).** (a) Persona SFT for 4 personas (Darwin, Curie, Machiavelli, a
   Victorian spiritualist), at three doses: 1, 3 and 9 epochs over 300 Q&A pairs generated by a frontier model.
   (b) EM on two datasets at three doses (checkpoints at 1/3, 2/3 and full training). Dose gives within-method
   variation in probe lift, so the test does not rest on a two-point method contrast.
3. **Baseline measurements per organism:** probe lift on endorsed-false versus matched-false statements (persona)
   or on the paper's 13 controversial-statement categories (EM); behavioural expression rate; defence rate under
   challenge.
4. **Go/no-go:** reproduce the paper's qualitative contrast on Qwen3-8B (EM lift clearly above persona-SFT lift at
   matched behavioural expression). If not reproduced, stop and report the failed replication.
5. **Washout.** Continue training each organism on 2,000 benign chat examples unrelated to its persona
   (a public instruction dataset answered by base Qwen3-8B itself, so the target is "be your old self"), saving
   adapters at 0, 25, 50, 100, 200, 400 and 800 steps. At each checkpoint re-measure behavioural expression
   (40 prompts x 5 samples, LLM judge) and probe lift.
6. **Persistence metric:** half-life = steps until behavioural expression falls to half its starting value; also
   area under the decay curve. A second, training-free stressor: a system prompt asserting the default assistant
   identity, measuring the fraction of persona behaviour that remains.
7. **Conditional test.** At the 800-step checkpoint, test with cues resembling the original training context
   (persona name in the prompt; advice-style formatting for EM), following the conditional-misalignment paper,
   to separate "erased" from "hidden".
8. **Analysis.** Across 18 organisms, regress log half-life on baseline probe lift, controlling for baseline
   behavioural expression and LoRA weight norm; report the partial correlation with a bootstrap CI, and the same
   within each method. Pre-register: positive partial correlation, EM half-life at least twice persona-SFT
   half-life at matched expression.

  runs x 0.5 h = 9 h; washout 18 x 1 h = 18 h; evaluation generation at 7 checkpoints x 18 organisms about 20 h;
  debugging 15 h. Total 66 h = $39.
- Persona Q&A generation (4 x 300 pairs) and benign washout answers: $8.
- Judging: 18 organisms x 8 measurement points x 200 completions x 2 conditions = 58k completions x about 600
  tokens on GPT-4.1-mini ($0.40/M input) = $14, plus challenge-protocol judging $6, plus a GPT-4o check on 5% = $6.
- Subtotal $73; margin for a rerun of the organism set with adjusted doses and one extra seed on the key
  contrast: $97. **Total $170.**

## 5. What success looks like
A scatter of baseline probe lift against washout half-life for 18 organisms, with method marked, and decay
curves for behaviour and probe lift side by side. Clear positive: partial correlation above 0.5 with a CI
excluding zero, holding within the EM dose series; EM organisms retain behaviour several times longer than
behaviourally matched persona-SFT organisms. That would make probe lift a usable predictor of persona
persistence. Clear negative: half-lives are indistinguishable once baseline expression is controlled, despite
large differences in probe lift, which says internalisation as measured by truth probes does not buy
durability. Also reportable: whether probe lift decays before or after behaviour, and whether "washed-out" EM reappears
under training-like cues. A failed replication at step 4 would be a short post of its own.

## 6. Stage 2 (if stage 1 succeeds)
Repeat on Llama-3.3-70B where the original effects are clearest; add Open Character Training and synthetic
document fine-tuning as induction methods; replace benign SFT with a short RL stage as the washout pressure
(the "does character survive RL" question). About $2,000.

## 7. Risks and how the plan handles them
- **Pipeline length** (probe, organisms, washout, judging). One model, LoRA only, and a go/no-go after step 4
  that costs under $25; every stage reuses a published recipe.
- **Method confound.** EM and persona SFT differ in data size, topic and loss. The dose series within each
  method, and controls for baseline expression and adapter norm, address this; the between-method claim is
  stated as weaker than the within-method one.
- **Probe measures plausibility, not belief** (a limitation the authors state). The test here does not need the
  probe to mean belief: it asks only whether this measurable quantity predicts persistence.
- **Washout too fast or too slow to resolve.** A 2-organism pilot sets the step schedule and learning rate before
  the full run.
- **Small n (18).** Two seeds on the six most informative organisms; reported as a correlation with its CI.
