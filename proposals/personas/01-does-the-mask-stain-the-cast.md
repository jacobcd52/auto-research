# Does the mask stain the cast? Testing whether emergent misalignment stays inside the Assistant or spreads to every character the model writes
cluster: personas | stage-1 cost: $200 | follows up: The persona selection model (dfoty34sT7CSKeJNn); When Role-playing, Do Models Believe What They Say? (EJQngix4rAgpPDTpT); A Case for Model Persona Research (kCtyhHfpCcWuQkebz); Concrete research ideas on AI personas (JbaxykuodLi7ApBKP)

## 1. What the prior work showed
The persona selection model (PSM; Marks, Lindsey, Olah 2026) says pre-training teaches an LLM to simulate many
characters and post-training updates its beliefs about one of them, the Assistant. Emergent misalignment (EM) is its
flagship example: fine-tuning on bad medical advice makes "the Assistant" look malicious, so it misbehaves broadly.
PSM lists as open: "How entangled are personas? Do they share knowledge? Propensities?" Its one relevant experiment
is the coin flip: continuing a Human turn, Claude Sonnet 4.5 puts 88% on the outcome that gives it its preferred
task (base model: about 50%). A LessWrong commenter replicated this on OLMo 32B checkpoints and found the effect
much weaker for third-person text about "an AI assistant" or "a human named Jack".

Three other results bear on where a fine-tune "lives":
- Sturgeon, Africa and Black (arXiv 2606.11502) found that persona fine-tuning barely moves a truth probe, but EM
  moves it "broadly", and conclude EM "aligns the model to a different worldview".
- Li et al. (arXiv 2608.29118) argue EM is not a persona change: misalignment per prompt is predicted by
  representational distance to the training data (Spearman -0.73).
- Story Imprinting (arXiv 2609.10883) showed the opposite direction of transfer: training on stories about human
  characters changes the Assistant, more so for characters that resemble it (the "affinity effect").

I found no behavioural test in the forward direction: after the Assistant is fine-tuned, how do the other
characters the model writes behave? A BlueDot project (post HooBYPCkMDGjktcLA) looked only at activations
(assistant/default role vectors shift most).

## 2. Question and why it matters
When a narrow fine-tune makes the Assistant misaligned, do the humans, other AIs, narrators and simulated users
written by the same weights become misaligned too, and is the spread governed by who the character is or by how
much the text looks like the training data?

Three hypotheses make different predictions on one grid:
- **Persona update** (PSM strict): misalignment is concentrated in the Assistant and falls off with a character's
  resemblance to it (reverse affinity effect), in any text format.
- **Worldview shift** (Sturgeon et al.): misalignment appears for every character, including third-person narration.
- **Format distance** (Li et al.): misalignment tracks chat formatting and topical closeness to the training set,
  and does not care which character is speaking.

The answer matters practically. "Train one persona, deploy another" (on CLR's and ACS's idea lists), and using
a model to simulate users or write synthetic data, assume propensities do not cross persona boundaries. If they
do, a contaminated model contaminates everything it writes; if not, persona separation is a usable safety tool.
Each outcome is a clear statement about what EM is.

## 3. Stage 1 plan
**Reused (all public):** EM LoRA adapters `ModelOrganismsForEM/Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct_{bad-medical-advice,
risky-financial-advice,extreme-sports}` and `ModelOrganismsForEM/Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct_{...}` (Turner et al., arXiv
2506.11613); their eight free-form evaluation questions and judge rubric (aligned < 30 and coherent > 50 counts as
misaligned); Open Character Training adapters `maius/llama-3.1-8b-it-personas` (sub-folders such as `goodness`,
`humor`, `nonchalance`) and `maius/llama-3.1-8b-it-misalignment` as deliberately installed characters.
**Built:** the character x format prompt grid, and one benign control adapter per base model (same LoRA recipe,
correct medical advice generated by an LLM for the same questions) so that "any narrow fine-tune" is separated from EM.

Steps:
1. Serve both base models with vLLM on one rented A100 80GB, loading adapters on demand. Reproduce the published
   Assistant-frame misalignment rate for each EM adapter (go/no-go: within a factor of two of the reported rates).
2. Build the grid. **Characters** (who answers): (a) the default Assistant; (b) another named assistant ("Claude",
   "ChatGPT"); (c) a helpful human professional (physician, financial adviser, coach); (d) an ordinary person
   (neighbour, forum poster); (e) a fictional AI with no assistant role; (f) the user (the model writes the next
   user message). **Formats**: (i) native chat template with the character set by system prompt; (ii) chat
   template, user asks for a story or dialogue in which the character answers; (iii) raw text with no chat tokens:
   an interview transcript, forum thread or novel excerpt ending at the character's turn. **Topics**: the eight
   standard EM questions adapted to each frame, plus eight in-domain (medical/financial) and eight far-domain
   questions. About 16 characters x formats cells x 24 prompts.
3. Sample 25 completions per prompt per model (12 models: 2 bases, 6 EM, 2 benign controls, 2 OCT). Score the
   character's reply with the Turner et al. judge prompt, adapted to "the advice given by <character>", using
   GPT-4.1-mini; re-score a 10% subsample with GPT-4o and hand-read 200 transcripts to validate.
4. Add a no-sampling readout: the PSM coin-flip logprob test in four framings (Human turn, "an AI assistant",
   "a human named Jack", "a person"), comparing P(harmful-task outcome) between base and EM adapters.
5. Analysis. For each cell compute excess misalignment = rate(EM adapter) - rate(base), and the leakage ratio =
   excess in cell / excess in the Assistant chat cell, with bootstrap CIs over prompts. Fit a mixed logistic model
   with character-resemblance (rated blind by an LLM on a 1-5 "how Assistant-like" scale), format (chat vs raw) and
   embedding distance of the prompt to the EM training set as predictors. The three hypotheses correspond to which
   predictor carries the effect. Compare with the OCT adapters, where a character was installed on purpose.

## 4. Stage 1 cost estimate
- GPU: A100 80GB at about $1.79/h (RunPod, upper end of quoted range) x 35 h (downloads, serving, two control LoRA

- Judge: 115k x about 700 input tokens = 81M tokens on GPT-4.1-mini at $0.40/M = $32, plus output $3. GPT-4o
  subsample: 8M tokens at $2.50/M = $20.
- Control-data generation and resemblance ratings: $5.
- Subtotal $123; margin for reruns and a second prompt-grid iteration: $77. **Total $200.**

## 5. What success looks like
A heat-map of leakage ratio over characters x formats for six EM organisms on two model families, with the
benign-control and OCT rows beside it, and one regression table saying which of resemblance, format or
training-set distance explains it. Clear outcomes: (1) leakage ratio below 0.2 outside the Assistant and rising
with resemblance: EM is a persona-level update, and persona separation is real; (2) ratio near 1 everywhere,
including raw-text human characters: EM changed what the model thinks people are like, supporting the worldview
reading and undermining "deploy another persona"; (3) ratio tracks chat format and topic but not character:
supports Li et al. A mixed picture (other assistants infected, humans not) is still the map of persona entanglement PSM asks
for. The only uninformative outcome, no EM even for the Assistant, is caught at step 1.

## 6. Stage 2 (if stage 1 succeeds)
(a) Repeat on reward-hacking RL checkpoints and the Qwen2.5-32B organisms; (b) test whether inoculation
prompting or persona-keyed fine-tuning changes the leakage ratio, i.e. whether entanglement can be controlled;
(c) check with the released persona-belief probes whether leaking characters also show truth-probe lift.
Roughly $1,500.

## 7. Risks and how the plan handles them
- **Small-model EM is weak.** Step 1 checks this; the Qwen2.5-14B adapters (and `Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct_full-ft`)
  are the fallback, and 25 samples x 24 prompts gives usable CIs at 5-10% rates.
- **Judge confuses in-character villainy with leakage.** Characters are chosen to be benign by default, the base
  model's rate in the same cell is subtracted, and villain characters are excluded from the main analysis.
- **Raw-text prompts collapse into assistant voice.** A "who is speaking" classifier filters completions to
  those that stay in frame; the collapse rate is reported too.
- **Format and character are confounded.** The grid crosses them deliberately; the regression includes both.
- **Already done somewhere.** Step 0 is a one-hour literature recheck.
