# Do LLMs build telescoping belief cones over sources they infer in context?
cluster: belief-geometry | stage-1 cost: $150 | follows up: The Geometry of Nonergodic Composition (JfJ4WTRHmooPBWRFv)

## 1. What the prior work showed
**Nonergodic post (Ray, Riechers, Shai).** When data comes from a mixture of sources, each fixed for a whole
sequence (a "nonergodic composition" of hidden Markov models), the Bayes-optimal belief is the concatenation of
per-source vectors `w_c * eta_c`: the belief over source c's hidden states (`eta_c`) scaled by the posterior
probability that c is the active source (`w_c`). Each source gets a cone that grows as evidence favours it and collapses to the origin when it is ruled out ("telescoping"). A 4-layer, d_model-128 transformer
trained from scratch on a 50/50 mixture of two Mess3 processes (3-state HMMs over a shared 3-token alphabet)
linearly encodes this 6-entry vector: held-out R^2 about 0.985 after block 3, versus about 0.45 for an untrained
network. The authors note (footnote 10) that a model could instead keep each source's normalized belief plus a separate "which source" variable; the joint R^2 they report does not separate the two. No code is released.

**LLM follow-up that already exists.** Balcells et al., "Large Language Models Develop Belief State Geometry
In-Context" (arXiv 2609.17376, same group) prompt six open LLMs with 20,000-token sequences from 40 *single*
HMMs and linearly decode the belief state (peak R^2 0.83-0.99, with shuffled, random, next-token and short-suffix controls, plus patching and steering). They set aside "level 1" inference (which process); there is no mixture condition and no code link.
Kowalyshyn et al. (arXiv 2605.08405) mix random walks from two graphs in context and find both in orthogonal PCA subspaces, without a ground-truth belief target or a weight-scaling test.

**Not done anywhere I could find:** testing the telescoping prediction in a pretrained LLM, or the
cone-versus-separate-variables question in any model.

## 2. Question and why it matters
When a pretrained LLM must infer in context *which* of two sources is generating the current episode and *what state* it is in, does its residual stream hold the telescoping geometry, and in particular does the representation of a source's state shrink as that source is ruled out?

The nonergodic post is motivated by real LLMs (documents, genres, speakers as sources) but tests only a toy. This is the direct test in real models, with ground truth. It bears on persona and
simulator accounts of LLMs: does the model keep tracking the internal state of hypotheses it has discarded? A cone means discarded sources fade; a "cylinder" (state tracked at full scale regardless of weight) means the model runs parallel simulations and weights them only at readout. A clean failure (good mixture prediction, neither geometry) would bound how far the toy result transfers. All three are reportable.

## 3. Stage 1 plan
Reused: the Balcells et al. protocol (re-implemented from the paper; no code is linked); the nonergodic post's process and model spec; Hugging Face weights
`Qwen/Qwen3.5-9B` (their main model), `Qwen/Qwen3.5-4B`, `google/gemma-4-E4B` (all ungated; Llama-3.1-8B is
gated and skipped). Built: mixture generator, exact Bayes filter, analysis code.

1. **Process.** A "resetting" nonergodic composition: the context is a series of episodes of 96 tokens, each
   generated by source A or B (fair coin per episode), separated by a newline. Letters F/Q/V, space-separated,
   as in Balcells et al. Two source pairs: (i) the post's two Mess3 parametrizations (from https://belief-updates.pub/nonergodic-geometry/); (ii) Mess3 with a structurally different 3-token HMM (Arch family, Balcells appendix), so identity resolves faster. Two context formats:
   unlabelled, and labelled (first 150 episodes prefixed "A:" / "B:", later ones "?:"). Contexts of 20,000 tokens (about 200 episodes); 20 sequences per condition. Ground truth per position: exact weighted belief, `w` and normalized `eta_c`, reset at each newline.
2. **Behavioural gate (first half-day, about $10).** In episodes after token 12,000, compare the LLM's
   next-token distribution with the Bayes mixture predictor (KL as a function of within-episode position),
   against baselines that cannot infer the source: order-0/1/2 Markov approximations of the mixture, and the
   single-source predictors. Proceed with a condition only if the LLM beats the source-blind baselines and
   its KL is within a few times the 0.01-0.02 nats of Balcells et al. If nothing passes, try 40,000-token contexts once, then stop and write up the negative.
3. **Positive control in a toy.** Train the post's 4-layer transformer on pair (i) (about 1 GPU-hour), confirm joint probe R^2 near 0.985, and run step 5 on it: the analysis is validated where the answer is known.
4. **Probing.** Cache residual activations at all layers for positions after token 12,000. Ridge probes
   (regularization chosen on a validation split) trained on 14 sequences and tested on 6 *held-out
   sequences*, for targets: joint weighted belief, `w` alone, normalized `eta_A`, `eta_B`, next-token
   distribution. Controls: shuffled beliefs, random targets, short-suffix beliefs, within-episode position as a nuisance regressor, single-source contexts.
5. **Cone test (key analysis).** Bin positions by `w_A`. Within each bin fit the linear map from centred
   `eta_A` to activations and record its norm (the "radius" of source A's geometry in activation space), and
   the held-out R^2 of a normalized-`eta_A` probe. Telescoping predicts radius proportional to `w_A` through
   the origin and normalized-belief decodability that fails as `w_A` goes to 0; separate variables predict a
   flat radius. Report slope and intercept with bootstrap intervals over sequences, beside the toy's.
6. **Causal check.** Mid-episode, add the `w` probe direction (toward the other source) at the best layer and all later positions; measure whether predictions move toward the other source's predictor, against norm-matched random and shuffled-probe directions (100 episodes).

## 4. Stage 1 cost estimate
- 3 models x 4 conditions x 20 sequences = 240 forward passes of 20,000 tokens (assumed under a minute each
  on an 80 GB A100), plus gate reruns and one 40k-token retry: about 8 GPU-hours.
- Steering runs (prefix cache reused), probe fitting, toy training: about 10 GPU-hours.
- Debugging and reruns: about 22 GPU-hours.
- 40 GPU-hours x $2.09/hr (RunPod A100 PCIe 80 GB, looked up 2026-11-14) = $84; storage about $15; margin for a second round on the best condition $50. No API spend.
- **Total: about $150.**

## 5. What success looks like
- Clear positive: in at least two LLMs the joint weighted belief is decodable on held-out sequences well above all controls (R^2 above 0.8, the range reported for single HMMs), the radius-vs-`w` line has positive slope and near-zero intercept, and steering `w` shifts predictions more than controls. 
- Clear alternative: source states decodable at constant radius regardless of `w` ("the LLM keeps simulating the source it has ruled out"), contradicting the toy; arguably the more interesting post.
- Null: the gate is passed but only `w` and next-token features are decodable. Publishable as a limit on transfer of the toy result, given the positive control.
- Gate fails everywhere: a short behavioural note; low value, but only about $10-20 spent.

## 6. Stage 2 (if stage 1 succeeds)
More sources (4-16) to test "eventually sparse" subspaces against Qwen's public residual SAEs; sources with shared structure; scale up to Qwen3.5-27B; a natural-language analogue with synthetic authors. About $300-600.

## 7. Risks and how the plan handles them
- **LLMs may not learn two interleaved HMMs in 20k tokens.** The gate tests this first; the labelled format, the more distinct pair (ii) and one longer-context retry are the fallbacks.
- **Probe artefacts in 4,096 dimensions.** Held-out-sequence splits (stricter than the prior paper's), ridge with validation, the controls above.
- **Inexact ground truth** (the LLM's model of the sources is estimated). Probe only late episodes; report results against behavioural KL; the toy control shows the exact-ground-truth case.
- **Few low-`w` samples for the cone test.** Choose episode length so weights span the range; report bin counts.

- **Tokenization and scoop.** Assert one token per symbol before any run. Simplex owns both ingredients, but the cone-versus-cylinder analysis is new for any model.
