# Evaluator rubric

Score the (revised) proposal on three criteria, each an integer 0-10. Use the whole scale; most proposals should
NOT score 8+ on anything. Read the proposal, the feedback it received, and enough of the underlying posts to judge.

**Naturalness** — is it a natural follow-up, e.g. one the authors themselves might have done with a bit more time?
- 9-10: explicitly named by the authors as the next step, or the obvious missing experiment everyone asked for in comments.
- 6-8: clearly on the mainline of the work; readers of the original would immediately see why.
- 3-5: related but a side-branch; needs explaining why it matters.
- 0-2: contrived, or only loosely connected to the cluster.

**Success likelihood** — forecast how stage 1 will actually go when executed by a team of Claude Fable agents (not
humans) in ~2 days within budget: how likely is it to yield a result that would earn >50 LessWrong karma?
State a probability and map it: 10 = >=90%, 8 = ~70%, 6 = ~50%, 4 = ~30%, 2 = ~10%, 0 = <3%.
Count against it: dependence on code/data/models that may not be public, long fragile pipelines, effects that need
large n, novelty already scooped, results that are unsurprising whichever way they fall. Count for it: reuse of
working infra, cheap fast iteration loops, a result that is interesting in both directions.
Base rate: most LessWrong research posts by unknown authors get well under 50 karma.

**Interestingness** — if stage 1 succeeds, how much would the safety community care?
- 9-10: would change what people believe or do about an important question. 6-8: solid, cited, discussed.
- 3-5: a modest incremental data point. 0-2: nobody would care.

Also give your own final estimate of the stage-1 cost in USD (not just the proposer's), and a short justification
for each score (2-4 sentences each) plus the single biggest risk.
